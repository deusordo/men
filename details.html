    What we do

    Meet. Commit. Act. Return stronger.

    We’re organizing monthly gatherings where men can work through challenges, share practical help, and leave with a clear commitment. Between meetings, we’ll follow through on those commitments and work on projects that serve our communities.

    • Personal progress. Set a goal and follow through.
    • Practical support. Exchange skills, advice, introductions, and help.
    • Collective action. Choose something useful to accomplish together.

    A proposed service project

    Help a local organization put donated tools and supplies to use.

    The project would start with a community organization that has donated supplies it can’t easily track. A small volunteer team would sort and label the items with staff and leave an inventory the organization can maintain.

    • Listen. Ask the organization which items they struggle to locate and what would make them easier to find.
    • Agree on the work. Define one area, who will do what, and what the team will hand over.
    • Check the result. Ask staff to find a few items using the new list and revise it after their feedback.

    The approach draws on Rotary’s community assessment tools, which start with what local people say they need.

    The brotherhood

    Masters of their craft. Men determined to master one.

    Doctors, lawyers, tradesmen, entrepreneurs, engineers, educators, public servants, students, apprentices, and men pursuing a profession. We bring together experience and ambition to strengthen one another and prepare the next generation to lead, serve, and build.

    Show up. Contribute. Follow through.

    DEUS ORDO means Divine Order. We’re building a men’s brotherhood across races, ethnicities, nationalities, cultures, professions, and economic and social backgrounds. We welcome men of any faith or no faith. Members commit to shared standards of conduct while keeping their religious, political, and cultural beliefs.

    Our foundation

    Different backgrounds. Shared principles. One brotherhood.

    Catholic moral and social teaching inspires our approach to human dignity, the common good, solidarity, subsidiarity, justice, and stewardship. These principles guide how we treat people, make decisions, and serve communities.

    Ten principles

    • Human Dignity. Every person has inherent worth and deserves respect.
    • Integrity. Act honestly, keep commitments, and reject deception, corruption, and exploitation.
    • Responsibility. Own your actions and fulfill your obligations at home, at work, and in the community.
    • Brotherhood. Support one another through cooperation, accountability, mentorship, and practical help.
    • Service. Put our skills and resources to work for others.
    • Justice. Treat people fairly, protect the vulnerable, and prevent unjust harm.
    • Stewardship. Use money, time, opportunities, and shared resources responsibly.
    • Subsidiarity. Make decisions at the level closest to the people affected whenever they can act effectively.
    • Solidarity. Take responsibility for one another and work for the common good.
    • Moral Accountability. Our principles take precedence over financial opportunities, majority votes, and personal interests.

    Build together

    We want members to bring forward business ideas, offer professional expertise, make introductions, and mentor aspiring entrepreneurs.

    Proposals should first be reviewed for fairness, responsibility, and their effect on others, followed by a review of legal and practical risks. Any collaboration that proceeds should have clear terms and responsibilities.

    Shape our future

    We want members to help shape community projects, events, programs, and shared resources. Proposals must meet our foundational principles and applicable law before they can move forward. Formal voting procedures require separate approval.

    Questions

    Can I attend before becoming a member?

    Yes. Choose Attend as a Guest to request an invitation. Location details are shared by email.

    What should I bring to a gathering?

    Think of one goal you’d like help with and one skill or experience you could share. The planned format gives everyone a chance to offer practical help and leave with a next step.

    Do I need to share a particular faith?

    Men of any faith or no faith are welcome. Catholic moral and social teaching informs our shared principles.

    What happens after I send a request?

    We’ll review your request and use the email you provided to follow up. Membership and chapter roles require review.

    Can my group ask for help?

    Yes. Use the community support form to describe the need and the result you’re aiming for. We review requests against available resources.

    Is there a member lounge?

    A member lounge is a future goal. The ways to get involved now are to request gathering details or propose a community project.
